# Articles and posts by Monica M. Hernandez and Daniel A. Montero

This page is the second part of the [publication history](https://bcds-llc.github.io/publication-history/) of BC-DS’s work on the decision architecture discipline. That page records the framework papers by Monica M. Hernandez and Daniel A. Montero. This page records their articles and posts.

Business Consultants for Digital Solutions, LLC ([BC-DS](https://bc-ds.com/)) is a decision architecture firm in Rockville, Maryland. It works on how consequential decisions are made, held and revised, with clear rules for AI, and builds [Decisiontect™](https://decisiontect.com/), an app that implements elements of Solo Decision Architecture (SDA).

For each piece this page gives the date and what proves it, and where anyone can check it when the proof is public. It records the publications of BC-DS and its two authors only. It makes no claim about who first used the words "decision architecture" or "arquitectura de decisiones", or about anyone else's use of them.

All times are given in UTC, with Rockville time beside them.

## Which pieces are listed, and how they are dated

Each author’s table lists the pieces that use one of the terms "decision architecture", "Decision Architect", "decision architecture discipline", "Business Decision Architecture" or "Solo Decision Architecture", or the Spanish forms "arquitectura de decisiones" and "arquitectura de decisión". Each quotation is exact. The section "Before the models" lists the authors’ earlier pieces on decision-making, published before 26 February 2026, the date of BDA v1.00, that do not use the terms. The page lists articles and posts. Comments are not listed.

LinkedIn prints an exact date under the byline of an article, and that date is given here as LinkedIn prints it. LinkedIn shows a post only by its age, so a post's date and time are worked out from its LinkedIn identifier, the number in its address, whose leading part counts the milliseconds since 1 January 1970 in UTC. Worked this way, the identifiers give the right date for six articles whose exact dates LinkedIn prints. Both kinds of date come from LinkedIn's own records. The account's owner can edit a piece after it is published, and LinkedIn marks an edited post without saying when, so these dates are not independent time stamps like the freetsa.org time stamps of the papers.

The article on bc-ds.com is dated by WordPress's revision history, which records when each version of a page was saved. Only BC-DS can open that history. It was read on 6 November 2026; the article has been revised since it first went public, so the text on bc-ds.com today differs from the text first published, and the quotations below are from the first version.

The article on Influential Women prints no date. Its date is the one the page itself gives search engines, in its structured data, which also records that the page was modified later.

2. **PM World Journal.** The link opens the article, and the journal's page gives its date.

## How this page is kept

Each new article or post is added to this page as a row on the day it appears. No row is rewritten. A row taken off is named, with the date, in a note at the end of the page. The rows of Monica M. Hernandez's articles and posts were first published on the [publication history](https://bcds-llc.github.io/publication-history/) page and moved here unchanged when this page was made.

The text of each version of this page, its file articles.md, is time-stamped by freetsa.org under RFC 3161, as the publication history page is. A version cannot hold its own time stamp, so each version's stamp is added afterwards to the repository's folder `timestamps/`, in a subfolder `articles/` under the version's commit, with freetsa.org's request, reply and certificates. Each version is also saved to the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine.

To check a version without trusting BC-DS:

1. **The time stamp.** Take articles.md as it stands at that version's commit, and its time-stamp reply from `timestamps/<commit>/articles/`. The command `openssl ts -verify -in reply.tsr -data articles.md -CAfile cacert.pem -untrusted tsa.crt`, with freetsa.org's certificates from the same folder or from [freetsa.org](https://freetsa.org/), answers "Verification: OK" only if the text is unchanged since the stamp was made. The command `openssl ts -reply -in reply.tsr -text` shows the time in the stamp.
2. **The Wayback Machine.** [Its list of saved copies of this page](https://web.archive.org/web/*/https://bcds-llc.github.io/publication-history/articles/) shows each copy with the date the Internet Archive saved it.

## How visits are counted

This page sets no cookies. To count visits, it loads a small script from Cloudflare Web Analytics. For each visit, it sends Cloudflare the page's address, where the visit came from, the browser and system versions, and how quickly the page loaded. Like any request, it reaches Cloudflare with the visitor's IP address. It sets no cookie and, as Cloudflare describes it, does not track individual visitors. GitHub, which serves this page, keeps its own records of the requests made to it.
